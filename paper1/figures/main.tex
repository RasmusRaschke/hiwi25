\section{\textcolor{blue}{Figure Family F: In Paper}}
\textcolor{red}{Now combined with former figure J.}
The fixed non-standard parameters were:
\begin{itemize}
    \item  $\Delta t = 1s$
    \item $\phi = 10^\circ$
    \item Rolling Resistance on, Aerodynamical drag on
\end{itemize}
while the initial magnetic dipole moment was parametrized as \[
    \bm{\mu}(t=0) = \mu (\sin \varphi, \cos \varphi, 0)^\top
\] with polar angle $\varphi$ and modulus $\mu = \|\bm{\mu}\|$. For each modulus $\mu \in \{0.0, 0.1, \dots, 2.5\}$, the polar angle was varied for $\varphi \in [- \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}]$ in $\frac{\pi}{399}$-steps, yielding $26 \cdot 400 = 10400$ data sets. The goal was to measure the influence of the magnetic moment on the deviation of the trajectory from a straight path.
\renewcommand{\thefigure}{F1}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{FIGF1_combined.pdf}
        \caption{Final $x$-deviation w.r.t. polar angle and modulus. Figure (a) and (b) both show the $x$-value of a trajectory after $t=1 \, \text{s}$ depending on the initial polar angle. Each graph represents a modulus of $\bm{\mu}$, indicated by the color bar. The first ten graphs in (a) show that the trajectorial deviation incrases non-linearly with the angular misalignment between $\bm{\mu}$ and the $yz$-plane. As expected, the deviation is antisymmetric about the origin. For increasing values of $\mu$ and $\varphi$, the deviation saturates. In (b), the curves for the remaining moduli $\mu$ can be seen. In this range, the magnetic forces start to dominate over the gravitational force for high polar deviations. The extrema of the curves correspond to the point where trajectories begin to loop around the origin instead of following the incline.}
    \end{figure}
\renewcommand{\thefigure}{F2}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{FIGF2_combined.pdf}

It can be seen that in all cases, higher angular deviations correspond to stronger deflection of the sphere from a straight path. Increasing the modulus increases the curvature of the trajectories which evolve from almost linear paths to near-circular arcs. Figure (c) also shows the behaviour past the critical points of Figure F1(b) where the outmost paths form closed loops.}

\section{\textcolor{blue}{Figure Family G: In Paper}}
The fixed non-standard parameters were:
\begin{itemize}
    \item  $\Delta t = 1s$
    \item $\phi = 10^\circ$
    \item Rolling Resistance on, Aerodynamical drag on
\end{itemize}
while the initial magnetic dipole moment was parametrized as \[
    \bm{\mu}(t=0) = (0, \sin \vartheta, \cos \vartheta)^\top
\] with polar angle $\vartheta$ and modulus $\|\bm{\mu}(t=0)\|=1$. The goal was to analyze at which point the magnetic force, which aligns the magnetic moment with the outer field, is surpassed by gravity. 
\renewcommand{\thefigure}{G}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{FIGG_polar.pdf}
        \caption{Total $y$-displacement after $t=1 \, \text{s}$ for different polar angles (a) and associated representative $y$-projected trajectories (b). Figure (a) shows the total distance in $y$ the sphere has rolled after one second depending on the polar misalignment of $\bm{\mu}$ and $\bm{B}$. There are three discernible regions: For almost all positive and a small range of negative deviations, the sphere covers distances characteristic for downward rolling motion. In the negative regime and around zero, the sphere is remaining around the origin. Between these regions, there are two steep boundary regions indicating an aprupt change between downward motion and oscillation around the origin. Figure (b) shows the evolution of the $y$-coordinate in time for three representative trajectories as indicated by the points in (a). The non-downward motion corresponds clearly to oscillation around the origin, while the downward motion shows only very weak signs of oscillatory motion in the beginning. The intermediate curve displays strong oscillatory motion in the beginning which is then overcome by gravity.}
    \end{figure}

\section{Figure Family H}
These figures were generated with the following non-standard parameters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Delta t = 10 s$
    \item $\phi = 5^\circ$
    \item Rolling Resistance on, Aerodynamical Drag on
\end{itemize}
The magnetic moment at $t=0$ was again parametrized by 
\[
    \bm{\mu}=\mu \cdot (\sin \varphi, \cos \varphi, 0)^\top
.\]
The goal was to observe the behaviour of the magnetic moment vector in the rotating frame of the sphere. We considered two cases: Downward rolling motion for $\varphi = \frac{\pi}{5}$ and $\mu = 0.1 Am^2$ and looping motion for $\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}$ and $\mu = 1.5 Am^2$. We lowered the slope and $\mu$ for the first one to obtain slower rotational velocities, making trajectories slightly easier to interpret. For the plots of $\bm{\mu}$ in time, we decomposed $$\bm{\mu}=\bm{\mu}_\| + \mu_\perp \bm{e}_z.$$  The drawn out trajectories correspond to the $\mu_\|$-component, i.e. the projection of $\bm{\mu}$ onto the $xy$-plane. The origin of the projected $\bm{\mu}(t)$ is the centre of mass of the sphere at time $t$. The trajectories of the COM were also plotted in arbitrary units, since they have to be scaled up to be visible in relation to the length of $\bm{\mu}$.

\section{Figure Family I: Not for paper}
The non-standard parameters are
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Delta t = 10s$
    \item $\phi = 30^\circ$
    \item Rolling Resistance on, Aerodynamical Drag on
\end{itemize}
Goal of these calculations was to take a closer look at which inputs are close in orders of magnitude to the observed motion of the sphere. The idea is to assume that $\bm{B}=B_z \bm{e}_z$ and $\bm{\mu}(t=0)$ are almost perfectly aligned at rest, with a little polar perturbation in the $xy$-plane causing deflection of the path. To quantify the size of the perturbation, we considered $\varphi \in \{0, \frac{\pi}{20000}, \frac{\pi}{2000}, \frac{\pi}{200}, \frac{\pi}{20}, \frac{\pi}{2}\}$.

\section{\textcolor{blue}{Figure K: In paper}}
The non-standard parameters are 
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Delta t = 1s$
    \item $\phi = 20^\circ$
    \item RR on, AD on
    \item $\mu_0 = 1.0 Am^2$
    \item No initial deviation: If the moment and field are not coplanar with the rolling direction, the rolling causes the misalignment after a few time steps automatically.
\end{itemize}
The goal is to investigate the general behaviour of the ball in roughly realistic settings. We used the NCEI model to derive the magnetic field vector at $53.5501721^\circ$ north and $10.0013165^\circ$ east (Hamburg, Centre) with a mean sea level elevation of $25m$ at the 12th of May, 2026. The total magnitude was rounded to $5 \cdot 10^{-5} T$, close to the actual $50159.3 nT$ of the NCEI. The magnetic field in the East-North-Up-Frame is then given by \[
    \bm{B}=B \cdot (\cos I \sin D, \cos I \cos D, -\sin I)
,\] where the NCEI predicts an inclination of $I \approx 68.6^\circ$ and a declination of $D \approx 4.2^\circ$.\footnote{The geographical spherical system is quite counterintuitive from the phycisists standpoint: The inclination is the dip of the field, so $I>0$ means that the field points into the ground.}

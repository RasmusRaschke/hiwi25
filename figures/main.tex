\section{Figure Family D}
The following figures are the results of running the simulation for $\Delta t = 1 \text{s}$ simulation time with an incline of $\varphi = 10^\circ$ for all possible values of $\mu_x(t=0)$ and $\mu_y(t=0)$ in $[-1,1]$ for increments of $0.1$, meaning these are the results of $441$ calculations. 
\renewcommand{\thefigure}{D1}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{FIGD1_mag_mom_3d.pdf}
        \caption{The surface represents the $x$-value the sphere attains after $t=1 \, \text{s}$ as a function of the initial $x$- and $y$-components of the magnetic dipole vector of the sphere. Since the $x$-direction is transverse to the rolling motion induced by gravity, it can be seen as a measure for the influence of the magnetic force on the trajectory. The graphs at the $\mu_y x$-plane are slices of the surface for fixed values of $\mu_x$, and respectively for the graphes on the $\mu_x x$-plane with fixed $\mu_y$.}
    \end{figure}
\renewcommand{\thefigure}{D2}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{FIGD2_mag_mom_2d.pdf}
        \caption{These plottings are enhanced versions of the slices of figure D1, left for different fixed $\mu_y$ and right for different fixed $\mu_x$. Keeping $\mu_y$ fixed produces symmetrical deviations from the free rolling path depending on sign and magnitude of $\mu_x$, as one would expect. A clear transition from regular sigmoid behaviour to non-smooth responses occures at highly negative values of $\mu_y$, at which the ball no longer rolls down, discussed more rigorously in D3. The slice graphs for fixed $\mu_x$ on the right show a characteristic constant curve for $\mu_x=0$, as there can be no deviation from the purely gravitational path without a torque non-parallel to the $yz$-plane. All other slices display an asymmetry for positive and negative values. This is explained by the fact that for positive $\mu_y$, the direction of rolling increases the misalignment of $\bm{\mu}$ with $\bm{B}$, slowing down the motion and hence also the deviation. On the other hand, negative $\mu_y$-values cause $\bm{\mu}$ to align with $\bm{B}$, accelerating the motion in general and hence also the deviation in particular. A less regular asymmetry occurs for larger negative $\mu_y$-values, which is again the critical region where the magnetic force dominates over and acts against gravity, causing looping paths instead of downward motion.}
    \end{figure}
\renewcommand{\thefigure}{D3}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{FIGD3_mag_mom_traj.pdf}
        \caption{Visible are different trajectories of the sphere on the $xy$-plane for selected $\mu_y$-slices and all $\mu_x$-values corresponding to these slices. Here, the anomaly in D1 and D2 for large negative $\mu_y$-values can be seen immediately. For smaller negative and all positive $\mu_y$-slices, $\mu_y$ only controls the spread of the trajectories, while $\mu_x$ influences the deviation from a straight path. At $\mu_y=-1$, the magnetic force induced by the $\mu_y$-component as soon as the $\mu_x$-induced torque bends the trajectory out of the $yz$-plane overcompensates gravity and causes an upward motion. After that, the sphere moves in roughly elliptic loops.}
    \end{figure}
\section{Figure Family E}
The following figure shows the behaviour of the $y$-coordinate for different values of $\mu_z(t=0)$ in $[0,1]$, incremented by $0.01$-steps. The goal was to see the transition point at which the magnetic force overcomes the gravitational force, so $\mu_x(t=0)=\mu_y(t=0)=0$.
\renewcommand{\thefigure}{E}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{FIGE_mag_mom_z.pdf}
        \caption{The graph on the left shows the $y$-projection of the sphere's COM at $t=1 \, \text{s}$ as a function of initial $\mu_z$-values. There are roughly three discernible regions: First, the $y$-value decreases, which is expected as the rolling motion increases the misalignment of $\bm{\mu}$ with $\bm{B}$. On the right, the $y$-trajectory for $\mu_z=0$ is representative for this region. For increasingly high $\mu_z$-values, a transition phase occurs. The representing graph at $\mu_z = 0.95$ shows that in this region, the sphere begins to oscillate slightly at first, but gravity soon overcomes the magnetic force. For $\mu_z \geq 0.96$, the magnetic force fully overcomes the gravitational force which only causes small oscillations around the origin, as seen by the representing graph for $\mu_z=1$.}
    \end{figure}
